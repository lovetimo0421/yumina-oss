The AGPL-3.0 license covers the code. The CC BY 4.0 license covers the docs. Neither one covers the
trademarks. You get the right to use, change, and share the software. You do not get the right to
call your version Yumina.

## What you can do without asking

- Say your project uses Yumina, is built on Yumina, or is a fork of Yumina. Truthful statements
  about origin are fine: "MyStory is a fork of Yumina."
- Link to this repository and to yumina.io.
- Use the word "Yumina" in a compatible way in file formats and docs: "imports Yumina cards",
  "compatible with the Yumina world spec".
- Keep the copyright and license notices in the source files. You must, actually; the license says
  so.

## What you need permission for

- Naming your fork, product, service, app, or company "Yumina" or something that looks or sounds
  like it (Yumina Plus, YuminaAI, Yumina Cloud, and so on).
- Using the Yumina logo, or a modified version of it, as the logo or icon of your project.
- Registering domain names, social accounts, or package names that contain "Yumina" in a way that
  suggests they are official.
- Presenting your fork or a hosted copy of it as Yumina or as yumina.io, or in any way that could
  make people think it is run by, endorsed by, or affiliated with the Company.

If you run a modified version, give it its own name and its own logo. Say it is based on Yumina.
That is the whole rule.

## Why

People who type "Yumina" should get Yumina. A fork with the same name and logo that behaves
differently, or handles their API keys differently, would confuse them and hurt them. The
trademark policy protects users, not the code.
